Household income shows the combined income of everyone living together. Individual income adjusts this to reflect what a typical person in that household would have. The dashboard uses the square-root equivalence scale: income / sqrt(household size). Canada's average household has about 2.4 people, so individual income = household income / sqrt(2.4). This method is standard in Statistics Canada and OECD income comparisons.
How to calculate net worth

Net worth = total assets minus total debts.

Add asset values:
• Cash & savings
• Investments (TFSA, RRSP, non-registered)
• Full market value of your home and other real estate
• Employer-sponsored pension value (not already counted above)
• Vehicles and other assets (approximate resale value)

Subtract:
• All mortgages (subtract each balance once)
• Student loans
• Credit card balances
• Lines of credit, including investment margin loans

Use your ownership share of assets and debts in Individual view; use combined household balances in Household view.

Do NOT include your annual income.

Example: a $700,000 home + $100,000 investments − $450,000 mortgage − $20,000 other debt = $330,000 net worth. Enter the full home value, not equity, when subtracting the mortgage separately.

Pensions: include employer-sponsored pension assets (for example, a DB plan commuted/actuarial value). Do not include CPP/QPP, OAS, Social Security, or other government pension entitlements.

Cash Flow
Take-Home Pay

Your net pay after income tax, CPP, and EI — from all employment sources. This is the amount deposited to your bank account — what you see on your paycheque.

Don't include: investment returns, rental income, government transfers (OAS, CERB), or one-time windfalls. Those go in net worth, not cash flow.
Spending

Everything you spend on living: housing, food, transport, utilities, subscriptions, insurance, dining, clothing, and minimum loan/mortgage payments.

Don't include: new savings or investments — those go in the next field. Double-counting is the most common mistake here.
Savings / Invested

New money being put to work: TFSA, RRSP, FHSA, or non-registered contributions, extra mortgage principal payments (above your minimum), and emergency fund top-ups.

Don't include: minimum debt payments (those are spending). This is money actively building your net worth.

Projection Assumptions & Formula

Percentile baselines (median, 75th, 90th) are values and are projected forward to the year you reach the target age using a constant nominal growth rate of 5.0% per year:

futurePercentile = currentPercentile * (1 + 0.05)^(yearsToTarget)

yearsToTarget = target age - your current age. These projections assume steady real growth and do not model shocks, taxes, or behavioural changes; the savings projection uses its own return assumption.

Estimated Employment Income by Age (Individuals, )
Description
Shows estimated employment income by exact age, including median and percentile ranges, adjusted for the selected province and household/individual view.
Sources
  • Statistics Canada - Canadian Income Survey / tax-filer income tables
  • Statistics Canada - Survey of Employment, Payrolls and Hours (latest wage-growth bridge)
Methods
  • Exact-age values are modeled estimates. Statistics Canada publishes income primarily in age groups rather than for every single age.
  • Published age-group anchors are converted to a smooth single-year curve using monotone interpolation so the chart remains continuous without changing the underlying anchor values.
  • 10th, 25th, 50th, 75th, and 90th percentile series are estimated from published distribution anchors and smoothed consistently across age.
  • Observed wage growth is used to bridge the latest source data toward ; the final dashboard-year value is therefore an estimate, not a directly observed statistic.
  • Province and household/individual selections rescale the benchmark using the same methodology throughout the dashboard.

Estimated Net Worth by Age (Households, )
Description
Shows estimated household net worth by exact age, including median and percentile ranges, adjusted for the selected province and household/individual view.
Sources
  • Statistics Canada - Survey of Financial Security (2023)
  • Statistics Canada - Census homeownership rates by age
  • Statistics Canada - household balance-sheet / distributional wealth data
Methods
  • Exact-age values are modeled estimates. Statistics Canada publishes wealth in broad age groups, not for every single age.
  • Ages 21-34 are calibrated using the official under-35 SFS median, younger renter/homeowner wealth benchmarks, and age-specific homeownership patterns, then smoothed to provide a unique value for each age.
  • Older ages are anchored to published SFS age groups and interpolated smoothly; retirement ages taper gradually to reflect typical drawdown patterns.
  • The 90th-percentile young-adult transition uses one shape-preserving curve through modeled anchors to avoid a legacy splice creating an artificial peak and dip in the 30s. Exact-age percentile values are modeled, not observed SFS statistics.
  • Province selection rescales the Canadian curve using provincial SFS wealth levels.
  • values are nominal dashboard estimates derived from the latest observed wealth anchors and subsequent household-balance-sheet trends.
  • Individual-view wealth is an equivalized/model-derived estimate because SFS wealth is published at the family/household level.

Description

Sources

Median Income by Province ()
Description
Shows a provincial income benchmark for . The measure changes with the selected unit so the chart uses an observed source appropriate to that unit.
Sources
  • Individuals: Statistics Canada T1 tax-filer median wages, salaries and commissions (2023)
  • Households: Statistics Canada Canadian Income Survey median after-tax income of families and unattached individuals (2024)
  • Statistics Canada Survey of Household Spending, 2023 public-use microdata file (62M0004X; released June 2026) for estimated median spending
Methods
  • Individual values are bridged to 2027 using the dashboard wage-growth assumption.
  • Household values use the latest CIS provincial after-tax-income benchmark and a modest nominal bridge to 2027. They are not created by multiplying individual income by a household-size factor.
  • Whiskers remain modeled distribution ranges rather than directly observed province-specific percentiles.
  • The red marker is estimated median household spending: the survey-weighted median of total current consumption (TC001), calculated separately for each province from the 2023 SHS public-use microdata using household weights (WEIGHTD). All households are included; values are projected to 2027 at 2% per year.
  • This is a dashboard calculation from anonymized microdata, not an official published Statistics Canada median. Current consumption excludes income taxes, pension contributions, personal insurance payments and gifts.
  • SHS combines recalled expenses with annualized short diary periods. These estimates describe median annualized survey spending and may differ from median actual full-year budgets.
  • Spending covers households; CIS income covers economic families and unattached individuals. Their medians do not describe the same household or a typical budget surplus. The spending marker appears only in Household view and is not a cost-of-living index.

Housing Cost Burden by Age ()
Description
Shows a modeled estimate of housing costs as a share of gross household income by exact age. The shaded band represents an estimated 25th to 75th percentile range.
Sources
  • Statistics Canada - Survey of Household Spending (SHS)
  • CMHC - housing-market and shelter-cost indicators
Methods
  • Single-year values and percentile bands are modeled estimates. SHS age-group shelter-cost benchmarks are converted into a smooth age profile.

  • The age curve is currently a Canada-wide benchmark. Province-specific adjustments are intentionally not applied because available provincial shelter-spending shares are not the same measure as shelter-cost-to-income ratios.
  • The series is a dashboard estimate, not a directly observed SHS value for every exact age.

Discretionary Income by Age ()
Description
Shows estimated take-home income remaining after modeled household spending at each age. The shaded band represents an estimated 25th to 75th percentile range.
Sources
  • Statistics Canada - Canadian Income Survey / income benchmarks
  • Statistics Canada - Survey of Household Spending
  • CRA - tax, CPP, and EI parameters used in the take-home estimate
Methods
  • Take-home pay is estimated from gross income using the dashboard's tax, CPP, and EI assumptions.
  • Discretionary income = estimated take-home pay minus modeled total spending.

  • Province values follow the same regional scaling framework used for income and spending.

Savings Rate by Age ()
Description
Shows a modeled savings-rate benchmark by exact age. Negative values indicate modeled spending or withdrawals exceed new saving. The shaded band represents an estimated 25th to 75th percentile range.
Sources
  • Statistics Canada - Survey of Household Spending
  • Statistics Canada - household saving / balance-sheet data

  • The ratio is not separately province-adjusted.
